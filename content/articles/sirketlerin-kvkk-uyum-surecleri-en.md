The Personal Data Protection Law (KVKK) has led to radical changes in commercial life since the day it came into force. Companies must ensure full compliance with legal obligations when processing the personal data of their employees, customers, and business partners. Otherwise, they may face both high administrative fines and prison sentence sanctions under the Turkish Penal Code.

#### Obligations of Companies and Risk Analysis

The KVKK compliance process is not just a one-time contract preparation; it is a dynamic process that requires continuous auditing and the taking of technical/administrative measures. The basic legal obligations that data controller companies must fulfill are:

1. **VERBİS Registration:** Companies with an obligation to register in the registry must enter their data inventory into the system.
2. **Duty to Inform:** It is mandatory to inform individuals whose data is processed about how their data will be processed.
3. **Obtaining Explicit Consent:** Except for the exceptions specified in the law, the explicit consent of the person concerned is required for personal data to be processed.

#### Administrative and Penal Sanctions

Administrative fines reaching millions of liras are imposed by the Personal Data Protection Board on companies that do not take the necessary administrative and technical measures regarding data security.

In addition, acts of recording, dissemination, or acquisition of personal data unlawfully directly lead to criminal liability in accordance with articles 135-140 of the Turkish Penal Code and can cause company executives to be prosecuted with prison sentences. This situation makes KVKK compliance not just a financial issue, but a critical process containing direct liberty-restricting criminal risks.
